# Content
Topic: **Neuroscience**

## Overview/Introduction/Context
* Simply put, neuroscience is the study of the nervous system, ranging from how the nervous system develops to its structure and what it does. It covers the whole nervous system, with a primary focus on the brain. Neuroscientists focus on the brain and its impact on behavior and cognitive functions. Technology is essential to neuroscience because it allows scientists to study how the brain and nervous system function. Technologies such as MRI scans, EEGs, artificial intelligence, and brain-computer interfaces help researchers observe brain activity, understand neurological disorders, study memory and behavior, and develop treatments.

* Nueropixels Probes
  * Very small electronic devices that scientists place inside brain tissue to record electrical signals from many brain cells at once.
  * Neuropixels probes take advantage of CMOS technology to pack nearly 1000 electrodes onto a shank thinner than a human hair.
  * What it does : 
    * Record electrical signals from neurons (brain cells).
    * Helps scientists understand how brain cells communicate.
    * Can record activity from different parts of the brain.
    * Helps researchers study memory, movement, and behavior.
  * Comes in different versions, including Neuropixels 1.0, 2.0, and Neuropixels NHP.
  * Designed, developed, and manufactured by imec (Interuniversity Microelectronics Centre), a nanoelectronics research and development center based in Leuven, Belgium.
  * Cost: 
    * Neuropixels 1.0: ~$1,400 to $1,700 each
    * Neuropixels 2.0: ~$1,800 each
    * Neuropixels NHP (Non-Human Primate): ~$4,200 each

* MEG Machine (Magnetoencephalography)
  * A MEG machine detects tiny magnetic signals produced by electrical activity in the brain.
  * How: When tens of thousands of neurons fire together, they create weak ionic currents that generate measurable magnetic fields outside the head. 
  * What it does : 
    * Measures brain activity.
    * Helps scientists understand how different brain areas communicate.
    * Can help locate unusual brain activity.
    * Helps researchers study memory and attention.
    * Helps study real-time language processing, sensory perception, and developmental or psychiatric disorders.
  * https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&source=web&rct=j&url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.instituteforhumanneuroscience.org%2Fnews%2Fihn-in-omaha-world-herald&ved=0CBcQjRxqFwoTCPCOgbr475cDFQAAAAAdAAAAABB7&opi=89978449
  * Typically costs between $1 million and $6 million USD to purchase.
  * Multiple manufacturers. Some include MEGIN, Cerca Magnetics, Compumedics Neuroscan, and MAG4Health.
  * Scans must take place inside a magnetically shielded room (MSR) to block out environmental noise from external electrical and magnetic sources.
 
* Functional Near-Infrared Spectroscopy (fNIRS)
  * A non-invasive neuroscience device that uses light to measure changes in blood oxygen levels in the brain.
  * It helps researchers understand which areas of the brain become more active during activities such as talking, reading, thinking, and moving.
  * When a part of the brain becomes more active, it needs more oxygen. Blood carries that oxygen to brain tissue.
  * fNIRS shines near-infrared light through the scalp and measures how much light comes back. This helps estimate changes in oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood in the brain.
  * Researchers use this information to study brain activity.
  * Advantages:
    * Does not require surgery.
    * Does not use radiation.
    * Allows people to sit, stand, and sometimes walk during experiments.
    * Can be used with children and infants.
  * Cost:
    * Low-Cost / Light Clinical Research Systems: $10,000 to $40,000
    * Mid-Range Research Systems: $40,000 to $120,000
    * High-Density / Advanced Research Systems: $120,000 to $400,000+
    * Clinical-Grade or Integrated Suites: $200,000 to $600,000+
  * NIRx Medical Technologies is a leading manufacturer of fNIRS systems
