#### Hardware
While I was researching for softwares, and discovered the Allen Institute for Brain Science, I was exploring what the website had to offer and stumbled across a section where they talked about the hardwares they used during their experiments. [This page](https://brain-map.org/bkp/experiment) talks about the scientific instruments the Allen institute for Brain Science used for their research, and I found **neuropixels** to be interesting. Neuropixels is a very small electronic devices that scientists place inside brain tissue to record electrical signals from many brain cells at once. The second hardware I found is the **MEG machine (Magnetoencephalography)**, after viewing a website from [Institute for Human Science](https://www.instituteforhumanneuroscience.org/equipment). The MEG machine detects tiny magnetic signals produced by electrical activity in the brain and measures brain activity. The third hardware I found is the **Functional Near-Infrared Spectroscopy (fNIRS)**. I first started researching about this hardware after viewing [this website](https://www.biopac.com/product-category/research/fnir-optical-brain-imaging/fnir-systems/) by BIOPAC. The fNIRS is a non-invasive neuroscience device that uses light to measure changes in blood oxygen levels in the brain. It helps researchers understand which areas of the brain become more active during activities such as talking, reading, thinking, and moving. For hardware, I only had to search "Neuroscience hardware" to help me get started and find some hardware. After finding my hardwares, I searched them up seperately to find more articles and informative websites about them.
